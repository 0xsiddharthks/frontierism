# Reviewing relationships between notes

This method is shared by the Notion and Obsidian analysis skills. The selected skill owns reading, writing, and verification in its own destination; using this method does not authorize changes in the other application.

## Read for a relationship

Inventory the bounded collection, including its actual IDs/paths and existing connections. A course number or matching title is not an identity. Scan the whole selected collection to avoid choosing only adjacent or familiar pages, then read each proposed source and target completely. A summary or keyword hit can nominate a candidate; it cannot substitute for the target read. For large collections, keep a compact index of central questions, mechanisms, assumptions, and existing links.

Useful relationships include a prerequisite that makes an argument legible, the same mechanism under different conditions, a counterexample or contrasting explanation, an application, a historical development, or a methodological comparison. State the actual bridge and its limit. Shared vocabulary alone is weak evidence; the same word can mean different things in two notes. Distinguish causal, chronological, and thematic relationships. Do not invent a causal link from sequence or a consensus from repetition.

Prefer a small set of strong connections, often two to four per page, with fewer when the evidence is thin. This is a reading guideline, not a quota or pass criterion. Give the reader a reason to follow each link: what can they compare, test, or understand there? Avoid self-links, repeated targets, generic "both discuss X" wording, forced reciprocity, and duplicating previous/next navigation without an additional reason. Backlinks already provide a return route where supported.

Cross-course or cross-hub analysis needs that scope in the user's request. Within it, retain exact collection and page identities; never resolve a target by lecture ordinal alone. When the user asks only for links, preserve the note's argument. When deeper analysis is requested, separate a source's claim from your inference and cite the exact supporting pages. Research new facts only when authorized by the task, and cite the external evidence separately.

## Inline concepts

Choose a few meaningful phrases already in the prose whose destinations teach or develop the point. Read both notes before adding the link. Preserve displayed wording, surrounding citations, grammar, and annotations. Avoid headings, existing links, code, YAML, question text whose answer would be exposed, generic repeated nouns, and user-owned passages unless those passages were selected for editing. Usually link a concept once per note rather than every occurrence. Keep important comparative reasoning in the Connections section; an inline link need not duplicate that entire explanation.

## Private review record

For each proposed change, retain:

- Source and target stable identity, collection, and reviewed revision/hash.
- The source and target passages or section anchors that justify the relationship, plus evidence that both complete notes were read.
- Relationship type, original comparison/reason, and any relevant uncertainty.
- Exact placement and proposed section text or inline phrase; enough surrounding context to patch only the intended occurrence.
- Existing equivalent links, the decision to add/update/skip, and the final verified result.

Keep snapshots and records outside a public repository. Do not copy personal content or long protected passages into public tests. Source-derived material added during analysis still counts toward the active source limits; splitting it among connections or labeling it analysis does not reset those limits. Use original comparative prompts where possible.

Review the proposed set for usefulness and duplication before applying it. A graph with more edges is not automatically more useful. A strong result gives a learner several deliberate paths while preserving the ability to read each page on its own.
