# Layered source analysis page

Begin the body with **Executive summary**, followed by a visible link to the original source and a compact creator/edition line when useful. State the central question, answer, operative mechanism or reasoning, principal evidence, and consequential tension at a depth appropriate to the source. Do not repeat a large title or metadata table before the summary; native properties hold filterable metadata.

A useful progression is:

1. **The question and stakes:** what is being explained or decided, against which alternative, and why does it matter?
2. **Understand it from first principles:** define the necessary concepts and explain the causal, logical, or institutional step. Use a clearly labeled example when helpful.
3. **How the reasoning works:** locate the major premises, mechanism, evidence, qualifications, and transitions in the source.
4. **The crux and strongest challenge:** identify the decisive assumption, objection/limitation, source reply where present, and residual uncertainty.
5. **Implications and study:** explain relevant consequences, a few meaningful connections, and optional recall/application questions with folded answers.

Adapt or combine sections. An empirical paper can organize the explanation around design, measures, results, and causal limits. A debate can compare speakers' actual positions. A technical or narrative source can follow its mechanism or sequence. A complicated source deserves more than an inventory; a short source does not need an elaborate apparatus.

## Source record and navigation within the source

Record the exact title, creators, edition/release, publication, language/format, canonical link, and requested scope where known. Explain consequential access, transcript, attribution, or visual-evidence limits beside the affected analysis or in a compact coverage section. Filterable metadata also belongs in the live database's native properties. Avoid repeating a collection-wide provenance disclaimer on every page.

For long or segmented material, include a source map and detailed located notes when these help the reader return to its reasoning. Use official chapters, headings, timestamps, or printed pages where available; label analyst-created segments. Retain conflicting versions separately. Do not add audiovisual runtime, speaker, or transcript fields to unrelated readings.

Use verified source locations and short passage-opening phrases. Analyst block IDs depend on an exact extracted snapshot; identify that convention and never invent publisher fragments from them. Do not transfer timestamps or page numbers between editions. An original title, the analysis title, and a collection ordinal are different attributes.

## Source artwork as the page cover

For a new analysis page, use suitable original source artwork as the native Notion **cover** when available:

- YouTube or another video: the exact upload's thumbnail from platform metadata.
- Spotify: the episode's artwork; use show artwork only when episode-specific artwork is unavailable, recording that fallback privately.
- Articles and newsletters: the publisher's representative image, such as the post's `og:image` or lead image.
- Books: the cover of the edition being analyzed, when available.

Prefer a usable high-resolution image actually supplied by the source. Inspect its identity and quality; avoid placeholder thumbnails, unrelated recommendations, advertisements, or guessed image-URL variants. Save the image URL, source page/API, and any fallback in the private source record. If no suitable artwork is available, leave the cover empty rather than generating or substituting unrelated art. Source artwork is presentation metadata, not evidence that the source's visual content was reviewed.

Preserve an existing user-chosen cover unless the user requests its replacement. Set the dedicated cover field during page creation or a supported property update; a body image does not set the page cover. Inspect the rendered cover and crop in Notion. Keep page icons omitted and the executive summary at the start of the body.

## Attribution and learner content

Keep source claims, independently established context, your explanatory model, and your assessment distinguishable through explicit local attribution. Consequential quotations and factual checks need nearby source locations. Publish original explanation and brief necessary quotations within applicable limits rather than reconstructing protected material through exhaustive paraphrase.

Preserve an identifiable learner-notes section during updates. Use native Notion targets for verified internal links. A collection worker supplies proposed connections by source key; the coordinator owns previous/next links, guides, and final native link resolution after the complete identity map exists. Standalone sources do not need invented neighboring pages.
