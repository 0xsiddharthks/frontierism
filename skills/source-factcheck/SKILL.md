# Source fact-check

Determine which consequential claims survive scrutiny and whether the source's larger conclusion follows. The user's suspicion, the creator's reputation, and a claim's popularity do not decide the verdict. Examine unfamiliar or non-mainstream theories seriously, with the same evidentiary standards used for familiar ones.

## Resolve and acquire

Identify the exact source/version, publication date, requested scope, and destination. Establish two time frames: what was knowable when published, and what is known at the review date. Do not use later developments to label an originally accurate statement false without explaining the change.

Reuse the relevant acquisition guidance from [source-to-notion](../source-to-notion/SKILL.md): [video and reading sources](../source-to-notion/references/videos-and-readings.md) or [Spotify transcripts](../source-to-notion/references/spotify-transcript.md). Read the actual requested content completely where accessible. Capture metadata, source artwork, the raw extract and its hash, and the actual ranges read. Keep source snapshots, drafts, claim ledgers, and destination records outside public repositories.

Automatic captions are evidence of a caption track, not a perfect record of speech. Check consequential ambiguous wording against audio or visible source material where possible. Distinguish caption errors from errors by the speaker. Disclose unreviewed audio/visuals and partial access prominently; never invent claims from metadata or imply that reading captions means watching the video. Extraction does not authorize republication of the full source.

## Build a claim ledger

Break consequential assertions into propositions that can be checked separately. Preserve the speaker's actual scope and qualifications. Use verified timestamps, sections, pages, or clearly labeled analyst locators. Repeated claims can share an entry.

A useful private record contains the claim, source location, claim type, applicable date/population, evidence supplied by the creator, independent supporting and challenging evidence, verdict, confidence, corrected formulation, and remaining gap. Distinguish:

- Observable facts and attributed statements.
- Causal explanations and claims of policy effectiveness.
- Comparisons, superlatives, and claims of novelty.
- Predictions, interpretations, and value judgments.

Check every factual hinge needed for the central conclusion, plus other material assertions. Say which minor claims or production elements were not checked. Do not call a check exhaustive merely because the full transcript was read. Avoid a percentage-true score: claim selection and importance make such precision misleading.

## Follow evidence to its owner

Search for evidence that could support the claim as well as contradict it. Prefer the responsible data producer, original research, full judgment, enacted law, legislative record, contemporaneous archive, or original recording. Read the relevant passage and its qualifications; search snippets and secondhand summaries are leads. Trace repeated reporting to its underlying source so syndicated copies do not masquerade as independent corroboration.

An official source is strong evidence of its own rule, recorded count, or announced position. Its claims about effectiveness or motive still need scrutiny. Scholarship and journalism also have scope, methods, incentives, and limitations. When credible evidence conflicts, explain the disagreement and what would resolve it. A failed search establishes an access/search limit, not universal nonexistence.

Pay particular attention to:

- **Dates and agency:** distinguish creation, parliamentary support, implementation, continuation, and later amendment. Do not attribute a cumulative policy to a single government by implication.
- **Law:** distinguish consultation, proposal, enactment, commencement, enforcement guidance, and actual practice. Separate fines, imprisonment, preventive detention, and sanctions for a different offense. Distinguish an advocate's opinion, a court's holding, and issues left to another court.
- **Numbers:** preserve units, denominators, time periods, provisional status, and relevant exclusions. Applications are different from approvals; a flow is different from a resident population. Recompute comparisons when needed.
- **Categories:** check whether religion, citizenship, ethnicity, origin, residence, ideology, or criminal conduct is actually measured. Never substitute one category for another.
- **Causation:** a before/after trend or electoral result does not identify its cause. Check comparison groups, policy timing, selection, displacement, changed definitions, rival mechanisms, and evidence concerning the people actually affected.
- **Comparisons:** strongest, first, only, unprecedented, and continent-wide claims need a defined measure and comparison set. One counterexample can defeat a universal claim without establishing the opposite universal.

## Calibrate the verdict

Use plain verdicts suited to the evidence, with confidence recorded separately:

- **Supported:** the proposition matches reliable evidence within its stated scope.
- **Accurate with context:** the core fact holds, but a material qualification is needed.
- **Misleading:** factual elements create a materially inaccurate impression through framing, omission, attribution, or scope.
- **False:** reliable evidence directly contradicts the proposition as stated.
- **Unsubstantiated:** the evidence located does not establish the proposition; this is not proof of falsity.
- **Disputed / unresolved:** substantive evidence conflicts or the decisive source cannot be assessed.
- **Interpretation / prediction / value judgment:** identify the type; check its factual premises without pretending the judgment itself has a simple factual verdict.

Split mixed claims rather than forcing one verdict onto their true and false parts. Explain the precise error and give the strongest corrected formulation the evidence supports. Confidence depends on source quality, relevance, agreement, and access, not rhetorical certainty. Missing evidence should reduce confidence or narrow the conclusion.

## Test theories and framing

Reconstruct a theory's proposed mechanism, assumptions, testable implications, strongest evidence, and plausible rivals. State what observation would increase or decrease confidence. Distinguish an empirical explanation from the values required to endorse a policy. Do not dismiss a theory because it is unpopular, or assign unsupported ideas equal weight merely to appear balanced.

Look for category shifts, selective time windows, unsupported causal bridges, ambiguous success criteria, emotionally loaded descriptions, omitted legal challenges, and inevitability claims. Explain their effect on the argument using located examples. Analyze observable persuasion without claiming hidden intent, coordination, funding, or a conspiracy that the evidence does not establish.

Assess production provenance separately from factual accuracy. Generic narration, dramatic music, repetitive phrasing, or stock imagery do not establish AI authorship. An automatic dubbing disclosure describes those audio tracks, not necessarily the original script or narration. If AI involvement cannot be verified, say so; the reliability assessment should stand on the evidence regardless.

## Deliver and publish

Lead with an intelligible overall assessment and the corrections that matter most. Supply claim locations, verdicts, evidence links near the claims they support, qualifications, and corrected facts. Use a compact chronology when timing drives the error. Fold detailed evidence where useful for reading on a phone. Include serious competing explanations, unresolved questions, and the exact review scope; avoid turning a verification request into an unrelated study guide.

Use brief necessary quotations and original explanation within applicable rights limits. A claim ledger should support verification rather than reproduce the source through exhaustive paraphrase. Keep a record of consulted evidence and the relevant sections privately.

When Notion publication is requested, use the supplied destination, check for the normalized source identity, and follow [publishing](../source-to-notion/references/publishing.md) and [page design](../source-to-notion/references/page-design.md) for transport, metadata, original artwork, and verification. Let this skill's fact-check structure replace the ordinary study-page structure. Preserve the live schema and learner content; keep hubs in List views and omit page icons. Use the existing fact-check-scope option honestly and explain greater detail in the body instead of silently widening a shared schema.

Verify every changed page's full saved content, parent, metadata, links, supported blocks, and representative rendering. Report the result link, substantive findings, and material coverage limits. Do not change sharing or contact the creator or sender unless separately instructed.
